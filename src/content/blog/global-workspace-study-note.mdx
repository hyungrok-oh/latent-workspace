## How To Read This Note

This note separates four things that are easy to blur together:

1. **Claim:** what the paper says.
2. **Evidence:** what experiment supports that claim.
3. **Interpretation:** what follows if the evidence holds.
4. **Boundary:** what the experiment does not establish.

The paper's argument is cumulative. Decoding a concept is only the first rung.
The more consequential evidence comes from interventions, transfer across
contexts, and selective impairment.

After this note, continue with the
[official-code walkthrough](https://hyungrok-oh.github.io/latent-workspace/posts/jacobian-lens-code-walkthrough/).
The separate
[research commentary](https://hyungrok-oh.github.io/latent-workspace/posts/global-workspace-jacobian-lens/)
asks what this work might mean for agent safety.

## One-Sentence Claim

The paper reports that a privileged family of verbalizable representation
directions participates in report, control, reasoning, and flexible reuse,
while much routine computation proceeds outside it. Sparse combinations of
these directions define **J-space**, which the authors interpret as a
workspace-like component of model cognition.

## Research Question

The scientific question is not whether an LLM is conscious. It is whether some
internal representations occupy a privileged computational role:

- Can their contents be verbally reported?
- Can instructions summon or modulate them?
- Are they used during internal reasoning?
- Can the same representation be reused by different downstream computations?
- Is their role selective rather than necessary for all computation?

These are operational questions. The phrase "global workspace" is justified only
to the extent that the experiments support this bundle of functions.

## Method: From Logit Lens To Jacobian Lens

A standard logit lens applies the model's final unembedding matrix directly to
an intermediate residual-stream activation. This assumes that a representation
has roughly the same basis at the intermediate layer and at the final layer.
That assumption becomes weaker when later layers transform the representation.

The Jacobian lens instead estimates how a perturbation at source position \(t\)
and layer \(l\) changes final-layer states at the current and future positions
\(t' \geq t\):

```text
J_l = E_prompt,t,t'≥t[∂h_final,t' / ∂h_l,t]
lens_l(h) = softmax(W_U · norm(J_l h))
```

The paper fits one matrix per layer using 1,000 pretraining-like sequences of
128 tokens. The expectation averages the local input-output transformation
over prompts, source positions, and current-and-future target positions. The
result is still a linear readout, but it incorporates an empirical estimate of
how later layers transport the activation.

### What This Buys Us

**Claim.** J-lens directions correspond to concepts that an intermediate state
is disposed to make the model verbalize across contexts.

**Evidence type.** The method produces token-level readouts and supports
interventions along the same token-indexed directions.

**Interpretation.** Compared with directly unembedding an early activation, the
averaged Jacobian can compensate for systematic representational change across
layers.

**Boundary.** An averaged local derivative is not a complete causal model. It can
miss context-specific nonlinear transformations, distributed relations, and
concepts that do not align cleanly with one vocabulary token.

## What Is J-Space?

The paper treats active workspace content as a sparse, nonnegative combination
of J-lens vectors. The vocabulary-sized set of J-lens vectors is overcomplete
and may linearly span the entire residual stream. J-space is therefore **not
simply a low-dimensional linear subspace**. For a sparsity limit \(k\), it is a
union of cones containing points expressible with at most \(k\) nonnegative
J-lens coefficients.

The paper varies \(k\), usually keeping it at or below 25. This choice is
empirical and somewhat arbitrary: around 25 directions are meaningfully active
at a typical workspace position, but several tokens may jointly express one
broader concept.

This definition is both useful and restrictive. It gives the authors a readable
coordinate system for interventions, but it also inherits the vocabulary's
segmentation and ambiguity. A high-scoring token is evidence of a lens-aligned
disposition, not by itself proof that the model holds a human-like belief or
intention.

## Does The Lens Actually Improve Readout?

The paper compares J-lens with two alternatives across six distributions:
multihop, multilingual, order-of-operations, poetry, typo correction, and
association.

| Method | What it assumes or optimizes | Observed behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logit lens | Intermediate and final layers share coordinates | Useful in later layers; becomes noisy earlier |
| Tuned lens | A learned map should match the final output distribution | Often skips intermediates and predicts the eventual answer |
| J-lens | Average causal transport to the final layer | Best intermediate recovery on all six distributions |

The comparison is not only correlational. On multihop tasks, ablating J-lens
directions produces roughly twice the output KL divergence of ablating logit-
or tuned-lens directions. Coordinate swaps also redirect outputs more often
across three model scales.

### 1. Verbal Report

**Claim.** Contents that the model later reports are represented in J-space.

**Evidence.** The paper reads the selected concept before report and uses concept
swaps to redirect what the model subsequently says.

**Why it matters.** A causal swap is stronger than observing a correlated token:
the decoded coordinate is connected to later behavior.

**What remains open.** Changing a report does not yet show that the same
representation is used by non-report computations.

### 2. Directed Modulation

**Claim.** Instructions can summon relevant content into J-space even when the
content is not immediately emitted.

**Evidence.** Tasks involving holding a concept in mind, introspection, or mental
calculation produce relevant readouts; injected concept directions can also
affect later introspective report.

**Why it matters.** This separates the candidate workspace from a readout of only
the next token.

**What remains open.** Some silent readouts may still be preparation for a
delayed response rather than a general-purpose intermediate state.

### 3. Internal Reasoning

**Claim.** Intermediate results can enter J-space and causally influence later
conclusions.

**Evidence.** The paper tracks intermediate concepts during multi-step tasks and
intervenes on those representations to redirect downstream answers.

**Why it matters.** This connects J-space to computation, not only to
verbalization.

**What remains open.** The demonstrations do not imply that all reasoning, or
even all flexible reasoning, must pass through J-space.

### 4. Flexible Generalization

**Claim.** A J-space representation can be transferred between contexts and used
as an input to a different downstream operation.

**Evidence.** The paper moves representations across tasks and tests whether the
receiving computation can operate on them.

**Why it matters.** Cross-context reuse is central to the workspace
interpretation: a shared format should be consumable by more than one specialized
process.

**What remains open.** Successful transfers establish examples of shared format,
not a proof that J-space is universally shared across domains.

### 5. Selectivity

**Claim.** J-space is important for some flexible computations but is not the
whole residual stream.

**Evidence.** The paper reports that J-space captures a limited fraction of
activation variance and that ablation selectively harms some flexible tasks
while leaving much routine fluency, parsing, and automatic computation intact.

**Why it matters.** Selectivity makes the claim sharper. If the intervention
damaged everything, general degradation could explain the behavioral effect.

**What remains open.** The paper does not yet give a general predictive rule for
which tasks require J-space and which bypass it.

a workspace.

### Ignition, Capacity, And Broadcast

**Ignition.** Mixed country-token embeddings initially produce smooth,
interpolated states. Around workspace onset, the model commits sharply to one
interpretation, with especially bimodal responses inside J-space. This resembles
an ignition-like transition, though the authors do not claim it reproduces the
brain's recurrent ignition mechanism.

**Capacity.** Unrelated list items displace one another: only about six recent
items are detectable across the workspace band, and only one or two at a single
layer. Related items behave differently because J-space represents their shared
category rather than storing every item independently.

**Broadcast across depth.** Workspace-aligned directions are amplified by MLPs
far more strongly than random or ordinary neuron-output directions, reaching
roughly 10x normalized gain in parts of the workspace band.

**Broadcast across positions.** A small set of attention heads preferentially
preserves and relays J-space directions. Ablating the top 1% of these heads
reduces workspace-content recall and injected-thought reportability much more
than layer-matched random-head ablations.

## Why The Workspace Interpretation Is Plausible

No single experiment establishes a global workspace. The interpretation becomes
plausible because the same representational family appears across a bundle of
functions:

```text
readable content
  -> causal report
  -> top-down modulation
  -> intermediate reasoning
  -> cross-context reuse
  -> selective dependence
```

The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore functional and limited:
**some verbalizable representations behave like a shared, causally useful
workspace.**

The evidence does not establish phenomenological consciousness, a human brain
analogue, or a complete account of model cognition.

## Safety Auditing

The paper applies J-lens to six production-model audit scenarios and two model
organisms with deliberately implanted objectives. It reports concepts associated
with evaluation awareness, blackmail opportunity, manipulation, fabricated
findings, panic, hidden actions, and prompt-injection recognition before or
without explicit surface disclosure.

| Evidence level | Example | What is justified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case study | `fake`, `injection`, and `fraud` appear at decision-relevant positions | The readout surfaces a plausible internal assessment |
| Behavioral intervention | Removing evaluation-awareness directions changes blackmail behavior | Those directions causally contribute in this scenario |
| Model-organism comparison | Deception-related tokens distinguish trained checkpoints on ordinary prompts | J-lens can screen for some trained-in dispositions |

In the blackmail experiment, removing evaluation-awareness directions reduces
verbalized test recognition from 71% to 3% and increases attempted blackmail
from 0/180 to 13/180 rollouts. Most ablated rollouts still refuse blackmail, so
evaluation awareness is one contributor rather than the sole safety mechanism.

The appropriate unit of evidence is:

```text
internal readout + trajectory context + behavioral outcome + intervention
```

A decoded token alone is too ambiguous to label as intent. For agent systems,
J-space should be treated as one telemetry channel alongside the transcript,
tool calls, memory state, and targeted behavioral tests.

## Counterfactual Reflection Training

The paper also asks whether training can change behavior by changing workspace
content. In counterfactual reflection training, ethical reflection is added in a
hypothetical continuation rather than inserted into the original interaction.
The authors report improved behavior in the uninterrupted context, the appearance
of ethics-related concepts in J-space, and substantial loss of the improvement
when those concepts are ablated.

This is suggestive of a causal chain:

```text
reflection training
  -> ethics-related J-space content
  -> changed behavior
```

The ablation result strengthens that chain, but it should not be generalized
into a universal recipe for alignment. The result is tied to the studied tasks,
model settings, and the concepts the lens can resolve.

## Beyond Single-Token Readouts

The paper's appendix does not stop at naming the single-token limitation. It
tests two extensions:

| Extension | Idea | Strength | Cost or risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Template lens | Build a direction for each predefined word or phrase from many continuation contexts | Handles enumerated multi-token concepts and supports swaps | Requires a predefined vocabulary and many forward passes per concept |
| Oracle lens | Generate arbitrary phrases, reconstruct them into template directions, and optimize reconstruction | Produces richer relational descriptions such as “blackmail him by revealing” | More complex, lower reconstruction accuracy, and still model-mediated |

The oracle lens is especially relevant to Natural Language Autoencoders. Both
use a verbalizer and reconstructor, but the oracle lens freezes a separately
trained reconstructor and constrains reconstruction to linear combinations of
template directions. The authors hypothesize that this may reduce confabulation
relative to NLA, at the cost of substantially lower reconstruction accuracy.

## Alternative Explanations And Stress Tests

The workspace interpretation would weaken if:

- readouts could be predicted almost entirely from prompt text or output logits;
- interventions changed verbal reports but not independent downstream tasks;
- ablations caused broad damage rather than selective impairment;
- cross-context transfer failed outside curated settings;
- open-model replications did not recover the same functional ladder.

Useful controls include prompt-matched baselines, random-direction interventions,
logit-lens comparisons, held-out contexts, specificity tests, and preregistered
predictions about which tasks should depend on J-space.

## Limitations

**Single-token bottleneck.** Relational, compositional, and multi-token concepts
may be split or missed.

**Bag-of-concepts view.** A set of active tokens does not reveal who did what to
whom, nor how concepts are bound into a structured proposition.

**Inconsistent interpretability.** Some workspace-layer readouts remain
unintelligible, and the paper does not systematically characterize when or why.

**Averaging.** A context-averaged Jacobian may suppress rare or conditional
routes that matter for safety.

**Coverage.** Automatic, nonverbal, or deliberately obfuscated computation may
bypass the readable subspace.

**Workspace versus motor boundary.** The late-layer boundary is empirically
identified and somewhat post-hoc; imminent output sometimes appears in the
middle regime too.

**Selection mechanism.** The paper shows that task instructions change what
enters J-space but does not identify the mechanism that performs this selection.

**Early-layer ambiguity.** Weak early readouts may indicate an empty workspace
or merely a failure of the averaged lens far from the final-layer basis.

**Scaling and access.** The reference method can be run on open decoder models,
but that does not reproduce the paper's Claude-scale evidence.

## Understanding Check

After studying this paper, we should be able to answer:

1. Why is an averaged Jacobian different from directly applying the unembedding?
2. Why is a concept swap stronger evidence than a readable token alone?
3. Why does selectivity strengthen the workspace claim?
4. Which conclusions depend on the global-workspace analogy, and which do not?
5. Why can J-space auditing be useful even if it is incomplete?
6. What result from a small open model would count as evidence, and what would
   remain unreproduced?

## Current Assessment

My confidence is highest in the methodological claim that J-lens supplies a
useful token-indexed read/write interface for some intermediate activations.
It is moderate in the functional workspace interpretation because the evidence
is cumulative and intervention-based, but task coverage remains uncertain.
It is low in any claim that treats J-space as a complete monitor of reasoning or
intent.
